# Evaluation protocol

## Question and comparison

Does a guided digital performance-reflection workflow help developing cricketers interpret available batting evidence and choose better-justified practice objectives than unstructured self-review?

Use two distinct fictional case packets with the same structure. Each has six innings, five dismissals, a repeated outcome, a not-out and one missing shot detail. Packet A uses the CreaseCoach demo and Packet B uses a matched case with different scores, dates and dismissal labels configured in the browser beforehand. Confirm that causal ambiguity and task difficulty are comparable before recruiting. Present either packet as unsorted notes for the baseline or as structured information for the intervention. Assign orders A-baseline then B-app, B-app then A-baseline, B-baseline then A-app, and A-app then B-baseline as evenly as practicable. Use the same instruction script in each condition.

## Tasks and scoring

| Task | Successful response |
| --- | --- |
| Find the repeated recorded outcome | Names the outcome and gives the correct count and dismissal denominator. |
| Assess a claim about cause | Identifies the statement as unsupported by the records and gives a relevant uncertainty. |
| Locate supporting evidence | Points to at least two correct entries or notes. |
| Propose one practice objective | States a specific, feasible focus and gives an evidence-linked reason. |
| Record the objective and a check-in | Writes both in the baseline worksheet or saves both in CreaseCoach. |

Record correct answers divided by assessed questions, successful tasks divided by attempted tasks, elapsed seconds per task, and counts of wrong denominators, overlooked qualifiers and unwarranted causal statements. Time each task from the end of the prompt until completion, timeout or abandonment. Use the same timeout in both conditions and record unfinished attempts as such. Ask “How clear was it which evidence supported your choice?” on a 1–5 scale anchored from very unclear to very clear. Record ratings as self-report.

Score the objective against this provisional project rubric. **0** is unsupported or unusable. **1** is broad or weakly connected. **2** is specific and relevant to the available evidence. **3** is specific, feasible, cites evidence and acknowledges uncertainty. Prepare examples for each band before running trials. Where feasible, have a second reviewer score anonymised objectives and resolve disagreements using the rubric.

## Sessions and consent

Explain that participation is voluntary and separate from grading, ask permission to record task data, and let participants skip or stop. Use fictional case records and participant codes. Ask about batting experience and device familiarity at a coarse level. Record the actual sample and context in the eventual report. Retain only the data needed for the study, with access and retention agreed before collection. Keep screen or audio recording optional and obtain separate consent.

Practice effects and the artificial classroom setting may limit interpretation. Report performance on the assigned tasks and avoid generalising from a small convenience sample to real batting outcomes.
