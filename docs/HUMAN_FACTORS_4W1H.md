# Human-factors analysis

| Question | Current task and demands | CreaseCoach response | Open assumption |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Who | Developing batters, with different levels of cricket knowledge, device access and coach availability. Coaches or peers may discuss a clip. | A player workspace with everyday wording, optional technical details and an ask-a-coach path. | Players can enter innings outcomes independently. |
| When | Soon after an innings or nets session, during later video review, and before the next practice. | Record just the outcome now, revisit details later and save one check-in criterion. | Players will return for follow-up after practice. |
| Where | Grounds, nets, hostels and homes, with variable connectivity, outdoor glare and limited privacy. | Local media, offline shell after first load, labelled controls and a touch-oriented mobile layout. | The interface remains legible in actual field lighting and on low-end handsets. |
| What | Remember outcomes, compare incomplete records, distinguish observation from explanation and choose a feasible practice focus. | Dismissal counts with denominators, drill-down to the log, optional notes, and separate evidence labels. | The wording helps users resist inferring causes from a small sample. |
| How | Move from recall and recording to interpretation, then to an editable plan and reflection. | Log → Insights or Video Lab → Practice Plan → checklist and reflection. | A player can recognise why an objective was offered and adapt it to their context. |

The course overview defines human factors through the interaction of people and system elements and covers cognitive ergonomics, perception, demand-resource optimisation, accessibility of interaction and physical performance topics (PDF pages 5–9). Its user–work characteristics diagram connects each lens to a different kind of demand (PDF page 13, slide 14).

- **Anthropometry.** The interface uses reachable mobile navigation and large primary targets. Field tests could evaluate operability across hand sizes, grip preferences and devices. The prototype made no anthropometric measurements.
- **Biomechanics.** The explorer demonstrates descriptive movement for a bat using synthetic positions and orientations. A future device would require mounting and calibration, controlled recording, validation against a suitable reference, and a repeatability assessment.
- **Information handling.** The log reduces the need to reconstruct an innings from memory. Charts show n, sparse data stays visible, and the plan tags calculated, observed, hypothesised and simulated sources.
- **Functional evaluation.** Trace whether players can enter a record, find the relevant evidence and leave with a specific plan. Measure success, errors and task time.
- **Aesthetic evaluation.** Assess reading comfort, prominence of the next action, perceived clarity, and use in different lighting conditions. The design uses consistent type, plain-language labels and restrained colour.
