# Anthropometry in CreaseCoach

Anthropometry describes body dimensions. CreaseCoach records standing height, arm span, shoulder breadth, upper-arm length and upper-leg length in centimetres. A player profile describes one person. A clip record describes the person in that footage and has its own dimensions. This matters when two shots belong to different batters or camera views. [CDC anthropometry procedures manual](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/127207)

| Source in the app | Meaning | Classroom example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Directly measured | User entered a value measured on the person. | Standing height measured with a wall scale or stadiometer. |
| Self-reported | The person supplied the number. | A batter reports their standing height without a measurement session. |
| Projected | A reviewer marked a visible body span and a known reference in one frame. | A visible shoulder-to-elbow span with a ruler held at the same depth. |
| Fictional example | An illustrative value for the demo player profile. | Arjun’s fictional profile includes a 176 cm standing height. |
| Unrecorded | Appropriate information is unavailable. | Neither stock clip includes athlete measurement records. |

## Demonstrate the workflow

Show **Player anthropometry** on Home, then open the one-click comparison and expand **Clip A anthropometry** or **Clip B anthropometry**. The video subjects’ measurements are unrecorded. If you have permission and measurements for an actual participant, enter them for the appropriate profile or clip, with a source for each value. For a live measurement demonstration, use footage you recorded with a measured ruler in the same plane as the target body dimension.

For a frame estimate, pause the clip, open **Measure on frame**, select the body dimension and enter the known length. Mark two endpoints on the reference, then two on the dimension. The calculation is `reference length × body span in pixels ÷ reference span in pixels`. The saved record includes the time and reference. Both spans must lie in the same front-facing plane. A plane that turns away from the camera, marked points on clothing or padding, and a moving or zooming camera can change the result. A crouched batter’s image height represents their projected span at that moment. Standing stature requires the appropriate upright posture. The pose overlay illustrates kinematics and does not replace a body measurement.

Record the protocol alongside any data used in the report. Note footwear, side for limb lengths, measurement points, instrument and repeats. Obtain the participant's consent and report only the values appropriate for the presentation. For an evaluation, compare multiple frame estimates against a direct measurement in a controlled setup and report absolute error and how repeat results vary. Until those data are collected, describe the tool as a prototype for recording and comparing anthropometric information, with an optional calibrated projection.
